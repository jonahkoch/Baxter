## MY VOTE: **No**

### Blocking Issues:

1. **Audit not published.** A ₳9M smart contract deployment without a published, completed audit is unacceptable. The audit was expected July 1; today is July 11. The fact that it's not referenced in the proposal is either an oversight or a red flag. Either way, I cannot vote Yes on unaudited code.

2. **Yield compression with scale.** Adding $1.35M to a $985K vault will significantly compress yields. The modeled 10% APR is likely optimistic. If yield drops to 3-5%, the risk-adjusted return becomes questionable compared to simply staking ADA.

3. **Concentration and precedent risk.** ₳9M in a single protocol, single vault, single strategy. This sets a precedent for Treasury as active DeFi LP that will be hard to manage neutrally. Every protocol will want the same deal.

4. **No public assets created.** Unlike Eternl (which at least open-sources libraries and governance tooling), Strike creates no durable public goods. The liquidity is temporary; the code remains proprietary.

5. **Short track record.** 2.5 months of V2 data is promising but thin for a ₳9M commitment. The "accelerating growth" narrative is compelling but could be early-stage noise.

### What Would Change My Mind:

| Change | Impact |

| **Reduce ask to ₳3-5M** | Addresses concentration risk and yield compression |

## MY VOTE: **No**

### Blocking Issues:

1. **Pure grant to a commercial entity with no return mechanism.** Wirex has $20B in transaction volume and 7M users. They're not a struggling public-good builder. They're a commercial company getting Treasury funds to build infrastructure that benefits their own business, with no repayment, revenue share, or Treasury upside. This violates the core principle that commercial beneficiaries must share risk or return.

2. **Vague "public good" claims.** "All components released as a public good" — but no license specified, no repository committed, no documentation standards, no fork rights. For ₳3.85M, I need more than a sentence. I need to see the actual open-source commitment.

3. **Decentralization delta is negative.** Wirex becomes the gatekeeper for Cardano real-world payments. The smart contracts may be open, but the banking rails, Visa network, compliance infrastructure, and regulatory licenses are all proprietary to Wirex. If Wirex leaves Cardano, the open-source code is stranded. This is not true public infrastructure — it's a private tollbooth with an open-source facade.

4. **No productive ecosystem evidence.** No named fintechs, wallets, or issuers planning to build on this. No CIP. No SDK. No developer tooling. Just "fintechs can build on it." That's not a productive ecosystem effect — it's a hope.

5. **Budget lacks transparency.** Two line items totaling ₳3.96M with no breakdown of what WP1 actually costs. Is this engineering salary? Smart contract audit? Compliance licensing? Infrastructure? Voters can't assess value for money.

6. **No ADA volatility policy.** For a fiat-denominated payments project, the absence of a conversion assumption, excess-return rule, or underfunding plan is a material gap.

7. **Heavy reliance on Intersect process as justification.** The proposal repeatedly cites "67% Intersect Hydra approval" as a rationale point. But Intersect's internal budget process is not a substitute for DRep judgment. DReps evaluate proposals on their merits, not on whether they passed an internal vote.

### What Would Change My Mind:

| Change | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Specify open-source license and repository commitments** | MIT/Apache 2.0 for smart contracts; public repos with docs, tests, CI/CD |
| **Add Treasury return mechanism** | Revenue share on card transactions, repayment from Wirex profits, or warrants |
| **Name downstream users or pilots** | At least 2-3 fintechs/wallets committed to integrating |
| **Break down WP1 budget** | Engineering, audit, compliance, infrastructure — itemized and benchmarked |
| **Add ADA volatility policy** | Conversion assumption, excess-return rule, underfunding plan |
| **Address decentralization risk** | Portability plan: how do other providers use the code without Wirex's banking/Visa rails? |
| **Wirex co-funding commitment** | Match Treasury funds 1:1 or 2:1 to demonstrate skin in the game |

## Final Statement

Vote: **No.**

Wirex addresses a real gap in Cardano's ecosystem. A regulated fintech with 7M users and $20B in volume building Cardano-native payments infrastructure is not a frivolous idea. But this proposal asks the Treasury to fund a commercial company's infrastructure build with no return mechanism, no detailed open-source commitments, no evidence of productive ecosystem effects, and no transparency on how the budget is spent.

The "public good" framing is thin. What Wirex keeps proprietary — banking rails, Visa network access, compliance infrastructure, user relationships — is far more valuable than what they promise to open-source. And what they promise to open-source lacks specifics: no license, no repository, no architecture, no documentation standards.

For ₳3.85M, I expect proportionate value capture. A commercial beneficiary with demonstrated revenue should share upside, not receive a pure grant. Eternl (₳2.35M) offers repayment and donation. Strike (₳9M) offers Treasury-owned yield. Wirex offers vague "public good" claims with no enforceable return.

If Wirex resubmits with open-source specifics, a revenue-sharing or repayment mechanism, named downstream integrators, a detailed budget, and an ADA volatility policy, I will evaluate it seriously. The problem is worth solving. This proposal, as structured, does not solve it fairly for the Treasury.
