## Native UTXOs on Ethereum — Review

**Author:** Toni Wahrstätter (Ethereum Foundation)  
**Core claim:** Optional Bitcoin-style UTXOs could reduce Ethereum's permanent payment state by ~99.8%  
**Status:** Research post only. No formal EIP. No timeline. Depends on EIP-8141 (Frame Transactions, still draft).

---

### What It Actually Proposes

The problem Wahrstätter identifies is real: on Ethereum, every payment — even a one-time ETH transfer or ERC-20 send — creates permanent state. First payment to an address = permanent account leaf. Wahrstätter's fix: treat payments as disposable one-shot objects. Prove they existed from history, keep only a single **spent bit** in active state (~0.3 bytes vs 100-150 bytes per account entry).

Mechanically, it piggybacks on **EIP-8141 Frame Transactions** — a broader account abstraction redesign that hasn't shipped yet.

---

### Assessment for a Cardano DRep

**1. Technical Merit: Moderate-High for Ethereum, irrelevantly late**

The state bloat diagnosis is accurate. Ethereum's account model *is* wasteful for simple payments. But the solution — "let's add UTXOs as an optional layer" — is fundamentally a retrofit. It introduces dual execution semantics (account model + optional UTXO layer), which means:
- Wallets must support both
- Smart contract composability gets messier
- Verification complexity increases

Cardano's **EUTXO model already does this natively**. No dual track. No optional opt-in. The state minimization Wahrstätter is chasing is just how Cardano works by design.

**2. Validation of Cardano's Architecture: Strong**

This paper is, in a sense, an independent admission by Ethereum researchers that the UTXO approach is superior for state efficiency. Hoskinson's reaction ("they're copying EUTXO") is slightly overstated — Bitcoin had plain UTXOs first — but the *extended* UTXO model with smart contract support is genuinely Cardano's innovation. Ethereum acknowledging the problem but proposing a grafted solution validates the architectural bet Cardano made from genesis.

**3. Feasibility: Low confidence for near-term impact**

- EIP-8141 is still a draft. Huge surface area (post-quantum sigs, account abstraction, batching, fee abstraction).
- No scheduled timeline. No formal UTXO EIP exists yet.
- Ethereum's governance for core changes is slow and politically fraught.

Realistically, this is 3-5 years away *at best*, if it ever ships.

**4. Competitive Intelligence**

Ethereum's "Lean Ethereum" roadmap (also from Vitalik) is trying to tackle state growth from multiple angles. This is defensive positioning — they see the state problem threatening decentralization, and they're borrowing concepts from chains that solved it differently. For Cardano positioning, this is useful: you can point to Ethereum's research as evidence that the EUTXO model was the right long-term bet.

---

### Bottom Line

As a DRep evaluating proposals, this paper is a **data point for the "Cardano got the fundamentals right" argument**, not a threat or a reason to change course. Ethereum is retrofitting state efficiency onto an account model because it has to. Cardano doesn't.

If this comes up in governance discussions or treasury assessments, the framing I'd use: *Ethereum researchers are independently validating EUTXO's advantages but proposing a hybrid solution with uncertain feasibility. Cardano's native EUTXO design avoids the complexity of grafting two models together.*

Worth noting in your mental model, but not something that changes Cardano's strategic direction.

---

## Overall Assessment

**Verdict:** Strong framework with a few areas I'd tighten or push back on. Worth adopting as a baseline, but not uncritically.

## What's Excellent

**Layer 0 — Funding Architecture Screen**
This is the crown jewel of v14. The insistence that *"a high-scoring proposal is not automatically a good Treasury allocation"* is the discipline most DReps lack. The seven-step hierarchy (Priority fit → Capital need → Instrument fit → Value capture → Opportunity cost → Proposal score → Actual vote) forces the right sequence. Too many DReps start with "do I like this team?" and work backwards. This inverts that.

**Instrument-Fit Guide (Section 0.3)**
Pragmatic and specific. The mapping of use cases to instruments (grant vs. loan vs. revenue share vs. LP position) is exactly what's missing from most Treasury discussions. The "warning signs" column is sharp — especially *"Treasury pays for hiring, sales, or customer acquisition while the company keeps all upside."*

**Narrow Abstain Doctrine**
*"Abstain is not support"* and *"conditional support is not a vote"* — both are correct. The discipline of mapping analytical findings directly to Yes/No/Abstain without a "Yes, but..." escape hatch is good governance hygiene.

**Anti-Fake Metrics Gate**
The requirement that KPIs exclude *"subsidized, related-party, circular, bot, duplicate, giveaway-only, or manipulated activity"* shows battle scars from watching previous funding rounds get gamed. Essential.

**Score Override Discipline (0.5)**
The explicit table of situations where a high score still maps to No is the kind of intellectual honesty that prevents scorecard capture. *"High score but weak value capture → Vote No for private capture"* is exactly right.

---

## Where I'd Push Back or Adapt

**1. The framing is heavily commercial/investor-weighted**
The rulebook admits this in the scope note, but it's worth flagging: pure public goods (research, standards, security audits, education) get short shrift. The default posture is "grant can make sense" with a skeptical squint. For Cardano's actual Treasury mix — which includes a lot of infrastructure, education, and ecosystem coordination — you may want to calibrate the public-good lane separately. The framework *works* for public goods, but the tone tilts commercial.

**2. "Prefer No over conditional Yes" may be too rigid for iterative governance**
I agree with the narrow Abstain doctrine, but *"If a proposal would only deserve support after changes, the actual vote is No"* could be overly punitive in a young ecosystem. There's a difference between "this proposal is structurally flawed" and "this proposal is 80% there with a clear amendment path." The rulebook treats both as No. For Cardano's current maturity, a small "Amendment-ready No" category with explicit feedback might be more constructive than pure rejection — though I concede this could be gamed.

**3. The enforceability expectations may exceed Cardano's current legal infrastructure**
Large proposals must identify *"contracting party, payment gates, failure rules, clawbacks or refunds, IP/control transfer mechanics, and dispute path."* This is correct in principle, but Cardano's Treasury system doesn't currently have robust legal enforcement rails. Demanding enforceability that the chain can't practically deliver sets up DReps to reject otherwise-worthy proposals. I'd add a lane for *"best-effort enforceability with on-chain milestone gating"* as an intermediate standard.

**4. Opportunity cost is named but not operationalized**
The framework repeatedly says *"consider opportunity cost"* but doesn't give DReps a practical way to compare against unfunded alternatives. In practice, most DReps vote on proposals one at a time, not as a portfolio. Without a comparative mechanism, "opportunity cost" becomes a vague invocation rather than a real filter. I'd add a simple question: *"What is the best unfunded alternative in this category, and why is this proposal better?"*

---

## What I'd Steal Immediately

- **Layer 0 before any scoring** — adopt verbatim
- **Instrument-fit guide** — use as a decision tree for every proposal
- **Hard gates in Layer 1** — fast, brutal, effective
- **Evidence / Judgment / Confidence separation** — prevents false precision
- **Prior receipts disclosure requirement** — Catalyst overlap is a real problem
- **ADA volatility discipline** — too many proposals pretend ADA is stable

---

## What I'd Add or Modify

| Addition | Why |
|----------|-----|

| Amendment-ready No with feedback | For proposals that are close but need specific changes. Keeps good teams in the ecosystem without lowering standards. |
| Comparative opportunity cost prompt | Forces explicit comparison to alternatives, not just abstract "what are we giving up?" |
| Best-effort enforceability tier | Acknowledges current on-chain limitations while still demanding structure |
| Community/dApp builder voice | The framework reads like it was written by an institutional LP. Cardano's strength is its builder community — the rubric should capture grassroots value creation that doesn't fit commercial metrics |
