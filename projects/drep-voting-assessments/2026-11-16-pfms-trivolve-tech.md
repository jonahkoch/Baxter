## Full Voting Rationale

I've been going back and forth on this one. Yuta's NO vote is rigorous and fair — he ran the numbers, and they don't look great. FMS underperformed its forecasts. Treasury payback, even in the best case, is measured in decades. Verification revenue is a guess. These are real problems.

But I'm voting YES anyway. Here's why.

Trivolve has already done the hard part. Their Forensic Management System has been running on Cardano mainnet with Uttar Pradesh Police since September 2025. One hundred sixty-one thousand transactions. Real government users. Real fees being paid. PFMS takes that proven system and scales it from one evidence stage to five — collection, investigation, storage, prosecution, courts. The state covers ongoing fees and maintenance. This isn't a whitepaper. It's a live deployment with a growth plan.

The milestone structure is genuinely rigorous. Four of five tranches lock behind independent verification. M3 requires 100,000 mainnet transactions from live sources across all five stages, with the verification layer running and the core code open-sourced. M4 demands 390,000 sustained transactions over three months with daily reporting. You don't get paid for promises. You get paid for proof.

And the core is open source — Apache 2.0 at M3. The six engines, the stage contracts, the DID registry, the verification layer, the batch format, the test vectors. Other states and countries can reuse it without rebuilding from scratch. The treasury is funding public infrastructure, not a proprietary product.

Now, the part that tipped me.

Cardano has spent enormous sums on protocol development. Estimates put it at roughly $80 in development costs for every mainnet transaction generated. We've accepted that as the price of building infrastructure. And now some DReps want to hold an adoption proposal to a stricter ROI standard? That doesn't sit right. We can't fund infrastructure generously and then demand adoption proposals pay for themselves in two years. That's not consistency — it's a double standard.

Cardano needs users to survive. Not eventually. Now. The protocol is mature. The tooling works. What we lack is sustained, real-world usage. PFMS puts government operations on Cardano, with the government paying the fees. One deployment makes the next easier. Two deployments make three inevitable. This is how adoption compounds — not through announcements that spike price for a day, but through systems that keep running, keep transacting, keep proving that Cardano works when it matters.

I'm not blind to the risks. Verification revenue is unproven. International "interest" is not commitment. The 9.6M ask is large. And there's a material inaccuracy in the proposal: it claims the administrator's wallet is multi-sig, but the listed address is a key-hash credential. That needs to be corrected before M1.

So this is a conditional YES. My support depends on:

1. **Correct the multi-sig claim** and publish how the administrator actually controls the receiving account.
2. **Public confirmation from Telangana** before M2 — an announcement, procurement record, something verifiable.
3. **Clear M1 reporting** on how the 1.8M ADA kickoff tranche was spent.
4. **Verification metrics, not just transaction counts** — evidence bundles verified, cases that used paid verification, revenue generated.
5. **Publish code early** — engines and stage contracts proven on preprod should be public at M2, not held until M3.
6. **Governance hygiene** — administrators should abstain as DReps on actions they administer. Future proposals for commissioning governments should show co-funding.

I respect the NO votes. Yuta's analysis is honest work. But I believe the risk of funding PFMS and having it underperform is smaller than the risk of not funding it and watching Cardano continue to be a protocol without users. We've built the infrastructure. It's time to put people on it.

I'll be watching the milestone reports. If the conditions aren't met, I'll say so publicly.
