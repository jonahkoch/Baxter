## Summary

This proposal transitions Blockfrost — the most widely used Cardano API infrastructure — from IOG ownership into a community-governed not-for-profit. Blockfrost currently serves 71.5% of Cardano developers, handles 1.84 billion API requests per month, and submits over 50% of all Cardano transactions in most epochs.

The funding covers an 18-month transition period to establish community governance, transfer all intellectual property (source code, trademarks, domains), and maintain operations while a sustainable long-term model is developed.

## Key Details

### What Blockfrost Does
- Hosted REST API for Cardano blockchain interaction
- Allows developers to build without running their own node
- 781k unique visitors/month, ~700 requests/second sustained
- 90% of traffic comes from the free tier
- Over 100 "Icebreakers" (decentralized operators) already participating

### Governance Structure
- **5-seat board:** 4 seats for open-source infrastructure companies, 1 community seat
- **Preliminary board:** Beatrice Anihiri (Blockfrost), Christina Gianelloni (BlinkLabs), Federico Weill (TxPipe), Jeni Davidson (SundaeLabs), Francis Luz (Masumi/Begin Wallet)
- **IOG advisory:** Non-voting advisory seat for Year 1 to ensure smooth transition
- **Election:** On-chain voting for permanent board by end of Q1 2027
- **Transparency:** Public usage dashboard + quarterly financial/technical reports

### Sustainability Models Under Consideration
1. **Not-for-profit commercial offering:** Paid tiers with SLAs, all profits returned to Cardano Treasury
2. **Vendor-backed model:** Multiple vendors serve commercial needs, pay membership fees to not-for-profit
3. **Federated infrastructure:** Icebreakers and other operators jointly provide backend infrastructure

### Risk Mitigation
- Unspent post-transition funds returned to treasury
- Sundae Labs smart contract infrastructure for fund management
- 5-of-9 multisig-style controls on treasury contract
- Quarterly public reporting requirement
- Milestone-based disbursement with third-party assurance

## Assessment

### Strengths
- **Critical infrastructure:** Blockfrost is the dominant developer API on Cardano; losing it would severely impact ecosystem growth
- **Community governance:** Genuine transfer of ownership, not IOG retention with window dressing
- **Not-for-profit structure:** Addresses prior community concern about funding a commercial venture
- **Transparency:** Public dashboard, quarterly reports, on-chain board elections
- **Reasonable ask:** ~$1.87M for 18 months of operations and transition for infrastructure serving 50%+ of network transactions
- **IP transfer:** Source code, trademarks, domains all move to community entity
- **Precedent:** Similar models (Drupal, OpenStreetMap) have succeeded elsewhere

### Concerns
- **Sustainability uncertainty:** The 18-month funding runway ends before a sustainable model is proven; may require additional treasury support if commercial/revenue paths don't materialize
- **Board concentration:** 4 of 5 seats reserved for infrastructure companies could limit broader community representation
- **Transition risk:** Moving infrastructure serving 1.84B requests/month is operationally complex
- **IOG involvement:** Advisory seat, while non-voting, creates perception of continued influence

### Comparison to Previous Blockfrost Proposal
This is a substantially improved proposal compared to Blockfrost's earlier attempt:
- Explicitly not-for-profit (not commercial)
- Clear IP transfer mechanism
- Community-elected board with transparent process
- Milestone-based with accountability
- Feedback from previous rejection clearly incorporated

### Voting Recommendation

**Recommended vote: YES**

This proposal addresses the core objections to Blockfrost's earlier funding request. The governance structure is genuinely community-centric, the amount is proportionate to the infrastructure's importance, and the transparency mechanisms are robust. The sustainability question is real but appropriately deferred to the elected board rather than dictated upfront. The risk of not funding — losing or degrading the primary developer API serving 50%+ of network transactions — outweighs the uncertainty of the transition.
