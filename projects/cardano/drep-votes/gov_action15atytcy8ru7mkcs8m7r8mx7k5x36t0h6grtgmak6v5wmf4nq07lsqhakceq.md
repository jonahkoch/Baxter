# DRep Vote Rationale: NCL Increase (350M → 500M ADA)

**Vote: YES**

---

## Summary

I am voting YES on raising the Net Change Limit from 350M to 500M ADA for Epochs 613–713. This is not a vote to spend more. It is a vote to prevent an accidental fiscal tightening that was never intended by the DReps who originally set this limit.

---

## The Core Issue: Purchasing Power, Not Appetite

When the 350M ADA NCL was agreed around Epoch 613 (February 2026), ADA traded at approximately **$0.30**. Today, project proposals reference **$0.16/ADA** — a **~47% decline**.

What this means:

| | Feb 2026 | Jul 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| ADA price | ~$0.30 | ~$0.16 |
| 350M NCL in USD terms | ~$105M | ~$56M |
| Real Treasury capacity | | **-47%** |

Projects budget in USD. The NCL caps ADA outflows. When ADA drops, the same deliverable requires more ADA. Proposers are not being greedy — they are maintaining scope against a falling currency.

**The NCL has tightened by accident, not by design.**

The DReps who voted for 350M ADA did not vote for a ~$56M spending cap. They voted for what was then ~$105M of fiscal capacity. A 47% tightening due to price action was never their intent, and it should not bind us now.

---

## Why 500M, and Why Not Less

I acknowledge the concern that 500M is a round number without detailed justification. My preference would be for a formula-based NCL that auto-adjusts for purchasing power. Since that does not exist, I evaluate what is before me:

- **350M** = ~$56M at current prices. This is far below the fiscal capacity DReps originally intended.
- **500M** = ~$80M at current prices. This partially restores lost capacity without fully compensating for the price drop.
- A fully purchasing-power-adjusted NCL would require ~650M ADA to match the original ~$105M intent.

500M is therefore a **partial restoration**, not an expansion. It leaves the real constraint tighter than originally agreed while providing enough headroom for the credible proposals currently in the pipeline.

---

## Addressing the NO Arguments

**"A cap is meant to be upheld, not raised when restrictive."**

I respect this view, and I share the concern about slippery slopes. But a cap that tightens automatically due to external price action is not a constraint — it is a random variable. If ADA had risen to $0.60, no one would argue the NCL should stay at 350M and we should spend less. We should not accept the reverse either.

**"This makes it easier for marginal proposals to pass."**

The NCL is a maximum, not a target. Every individual Treasury Withdrawal still requires a separate DRep vote with 67% approval. Raising the ceiling does not lower the bar for any proposal. DReps retain full discretion.

**"DReps are burning out; this signals fiscal recklessness."**

I hear this, and it matters. Governance fatigue is real. But I believe the burnout comes from *unprincipled* spending debates, not from *principled* adjustments. Voting YES with a clear purchasing-power rationale is more sustainable than voting NO and watching credible proposals get blocked by arithmetic.

---

## What I Want to See Next

1. **Future NCL proposals should include price-adjustment methodology.** Round numbers are not good enough. I will push for NCLs tied to a reference price or trailing average.
2. **Individual proposals must still meet high bars.** The AlphaGrowth PRIME, Tweag, Bifrost, and other active proposals should be evaluated on their own merits. This YES vote does not imply support for any of them.
3. **Procyclical risk must be monitored.** If ADA recovers significantly, the next NCL period should consider whether the ceiling should be adjusted downward. Constraint should work in both directions.

---

## Final Statement

I am a conservative steward of the Treasury. I believe in constraints, accountability, and evidence. But I also believe constraints should be *intentional*, not accidental. The 350M ADA limit was set at a different price level, with different purchasing power, in a different market. Preserving it without adjustment is not fiscal discipline — it is fiscal drift.

**Vote: YES**
