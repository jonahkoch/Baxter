## Summary

Vote: **Yes.**

Mithril is not a speculative bet. It is live, working infrastructure that solves a genuine and growing problem for Cardano: the cost and complexity of securely accessing and verifying blockchain state. As the chain grows, full-node sync becomes slower and light clients become more dependent on centralized indexers and hosted APIs. Mithril addresses this with stake-based threshold multi-signatures that certify Cardano state in a trustless way.

This proposal asks the Treasury to fund **continued development of an open-source protocol** by a team (Teragone) that has already demonstrated years of capability through IOG-funded collaboration. The request is a transition from private (IOG) to community-governed (Intersect/Treasury) funding — a structurally sound evolution.

However, this is not an unqualified endorsement. The budget lacks itemization, milestones are not visible in the public metadata, and there is no ADA volatility policy. These are gaps I have flagged as blocking in other proposals, and I do not give Mithril a pass on them. The difference here is that the **public asset is already real and verifiable** — Mithril repositories are public, the protocol is operational, and the value proposition is not theoretical. The risk is execution and continuity, not vaporware.

I vote Yes with the expectation that Intersect and Teragone improve transparency in future funding rounds.

## What This Proposal Gets Right

### 1. Genuine Public Asset (Not "Public Good" Theater)

Mithril is already open-source. The repositories exist. The protocol is documented. The research is published. This is not a promise to open-source something later — it is already public infrastructure.

What Cardano receives:
- **Public asset:** Mithril protocol code, standards, and research (already open-source)
- **Public service:** Faster, trustless node synchronization for the entire network
- **Public learning:** Advancement in stake-based threshold signature schemes
- **Avoided loss:** Reduced dependency on centralized indexers and hosted APIs

This maps cleanly to four of the Five Forms of Public Return. The durability is strong: protocol code persists, standards enable interoperability, and reduced indexer dependency is a lasting structural improvement.

### 2. Proven Team with Demonstrated Capability

Teragone has worked on Mithril for several years in collaboration with IOG. This is not a team proposing to learn on the job. They have:
- Delivered working protocol iterations
- Published research and documentation
- Operated within IOG's engineering standards
- Built operational expertise specific to Cardano's staking layer

The transition from IOG funding to Treasury funding is a healthy evolution. It reduces single-entity dependency and moves core infrastructure under community governance.

### 3. Positive Decentralization Delta

Mithril directly reduces barriers to running nodes and verifying state:
- **Light clients** can verify state without trusting centralized servers
- **Full nodes** can sync faster without downloading the entire chain
- **Exchanges and wallets** can operate with lower infrastructure costs
- **Individual users** can participate with consumer hardware

This is decentralization infrastructure, not decentralization theater. It makes the network more accessible, more verifiable, and less dependent on a small number of hosted API providers.

### 4. Strong Oversight Framework

The proposal uses Intersect's standard Treasury management structure:
- **Sundae Labs smart contracts** for milestone-based disbursement
- **Intersect administration** with 5 admin + 3 leadership roles
- **6-member Oversight Committee** (Sundae Labs, Cardano Foundation, Dquadrant, NMKR, Sundial, Eternl)
- **Multi-signature requirements** for fund movement (2-of-5 admins + 2-of-6 trusted entities + 1-of-3 leadership for modifications; stricter for disbursement)
- **Public dashboard** at treasury.sundae.fi for transparency

This is the same framework used for other Intersect-administered proposals and represents a credible accountability layer.

### 5. No Prior Treasury Funding

Clean slate. Not a repeat recipient gaming the system. Previous funding came from IOG, which is a transparent and legitimate source.

### 6. Net Change Limit Compliance

The proposal explicitly states compliance with the 350M Net Change Limit (Epoch 613-713). This is a basic but necessary check.

### 7. Strategic Alignment

Mithril aligns with Intersect's stated pillars:
- **Pillar 1 (Infrastructure & Research):** Fully aligned — state proofs are core infrastructure
- **Pillar 2 (Adoption & Utility):** Fully aligned — faster sync lowers onboarding friction
- **Pillar 4 (Community & Ecosystem):** Partially aligned — enables community-built tools

The rationale is coherent and the alignment is genuine, not forced.

## Concerns (Not Blocking, But Real)

### 1. Budget Lacks Itemization

| Line Item | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Mithril Protocol Continuous Development | ₳3,699,440 |
| Intersect Budget Administration fee | ₳110,983 |
| **Total** | **₳3,810,423** |

Two line items for ₳3.8M. This is the same opacity I criticized in Wirex, and I will not ignore it here.

What is missing:
- Engineering team size, composition, and rates
- Specific deliverables and timelines
- Audit or security review costs
- Infrastructure and operational costs
- Research and documentation scope
- Contingency allocation

For future funding rounds, Teragone should provide a detailed breakdown. Voters cannot assess value for money without understanding what the money buys.

### 2. Milestones Not Visible in Public Metadata

The metadata references "milestone-based disbursement controls" but does not list the milestones. This means DReps must trust that Intersect has defined appropriate milestones without being able to verify them.

The Sundae Labs dashboard may reveal milestones post-enactment, but pre-vote visibility is limited. This is a structural gap in how Intersect packages proposals for on-chain governance.

### 3. No ADA Volatility Policy

For a ₳3.8M request, the absence of an ADA volatility policy is a material gap:
- No conversion assumption stated
- No excess-return rule if ADA appreciates
- No underfunding plan if ADA depreciates
- No clarity on who bears exchange-rate risk

Given ADA's volatility, this is a real risk. If ADA drops 30% between enactment and disbursement, does the scope reduce? Does Teragone absorb the shortfall? Or does the Treasury effectively overpay? The proposal is silent.

### 4. "Continuous Development" Is Vague Scope

The title and description frame this as "continuous development" without specifying:
- What features or improvements are planned
- What the 12-month (or other period) roadmap looks like
- What "done" looks like for this funding tranche

This makes it difficult to evaluate whether ₳3.7M is the right amount for the right scope. Is this maintenance, feature expansion, research, or all three?

### 5. No License or Governance Specified for Future Work

While Mithril is currently open-source, this proposal does not specify:
- What license future work will be released under
- Whether governance of the protocol will transition to a community process
- What happens if Teragone stops working on Mithril

These are long-term continuity questions that deserve explicit answers for Treasury-funded infrastructure.

The score is borderline because of the transparency gaps. However, I apply a **contextual override** for the following reasons:

1. **The public asset is already verifiable.** Mithril is not a promise; it is working code. The risk is not vaporware.
2. **The team is proven.** Teragone has delivered under IOG funding for years.
3. **The decentralization delta is unambiguously positive.** This is infrastructure that makes the network more accessible and less centralized.
4. **The funding transition is structurally sound.** Moving from IOG to Treasury reduces single-entity dependency.

The transparency gaps are real and I will not dismiss them. But they are process failures (Intersect's packaging) rather than substantive failures of the proposal itself. I vote Yes with the expectation that these gaps are closed in future rounds.

## Final Statement

Vote: **Yes.**

Mithril is critical infrastructure for Cardano's scaling and decentralization. It is already an open-source public asset, maintained by a proven team, with a clear and positive decentralization delta. The request to transition funding from IOG to the Treasury represents a healthy evolution toward community-governed infrastructure.

The concerns I raise — budget opacity, invisible milestones, no ADA volatility policy — are genuine and would be blocking in a more speculative or commercial proposal. They are not blocking here because the underlying value is already demonstrated and verifiable. But they are not forgotten. I expect improvement in future proposals.

If this were a new team with no track record, or a closed-source commercial product, these gaps would lead me to No or Abstain. For Mithril, the context warrants a qualified Yes.
