## Summary

Funds Phase 2 of a production Cardano-native ticketing platform. **Phase 1 is already live on mainnet** — on-chain event creation, CIP-68 NFT ticket minting, transfers, and attendance verification — funded entirely by Sellout's own capital ($130K+ invested). Phase 2 adds a secondary marketplace with on-chain royalty enforcement, anti-scalping controls, custodial wallet onboarding for 200,000+ existing Web2 users, organizer tools, security audit, and a professional launch campaign anchored by the **Yellowstone Club 2026 concert series** ($6M+ projected ticket revenue).

**Key differentiator:** Revenue-share repayment to Treasury — 25% of marketplace/royalty fees until $1.09M repaid, then 5% permanently.

### Layer 1 — Proposal Quality

**Section A: Basics**
- ✓ Accountable applicants with proven track records
- ✓ Clear ask with itemized budget
- ✓ Detailed milestone structure with acceptance criteria
- ✓ Prior funding disclosed (self-funded Phase 1, past Catalyst/Intersect grants for Anvil)

- ✓ Cryptographic signatures from both Anvil and Intersect

**Section B: Value & Impact**
- ⚠ Public asset quality: Marketplace contracts and CIP-68 standards are potentially reusable, but no explicit open-source commitment
- ✓ Productive public value: Protocol fees, demonstrated enterprise viability, reusable case study
- ✓ Additionality: Phase 1 exists and works. Treasury funds the *incremental* build to scale.
- ✓ Critical gap: Cardano has few enterprise integrations with real user bases. This is a genuine gap.
- ✓ Counterfactual harm: Without funding, Sellout likely continues as a Web2 platform; the Cardano integration stalls or remains limited
- ⚠ Retained impact: Depends on open-sourcing and Sellout's continued operation. Revenue-share provides ongoing return.

**Section C: Execution & Accountability**
- ✓ Strong team evidence: Anvil (250+ Cardano projects, Wayup marketplace, 456K assets minted) + Sellout (200K users, operational ticketing platform)
- ✓ Milestones: 5 phases with clear deliverables, acceptance criteria, and verification methods
- ✓ Independent verification: Third-party security audit, public on-chain metrics dashboard, quarterly reporting
- ✓ Anti-gaming: Milestone gating, contingency return, revenue-share creates alignment
- ✓ Enforceability: Sundae Labs TRSC/PSSC with 6-member Oversight Committee, contractual revenue-share obligation

**Section D: Commercial**
- ⚠ This is a commercial proposal with public-good spillover. Sellout is a for-profit platform.
- ✓ Revenue-share mitigates the commercial nature: Treasury gets paid back (25% until $1.09M, then 5% ongoing)
- ⚠ No equity or token for Treasury — rev-share is cleaner but capped at fee revenue, not platform value
- ⚠ If Sellout succeeds and is acquired, Treasury gets no upside beyond the rev-share

**Section E: Marketing & Adoption** — **Relevant here**. The proposal includes a $170K marketing budget (3 trade shows + Big Storm campaign). Trade shows (NIVA, INTIX, Pollstar Live!) target the exact ticketing industry professionals who would adopt or white-label this platform. This is targeted, high-value marketing.

**Section F: Decentralization Delta** — **Slightly Positive**. Adds on-chain activity, new wallets, and protocol fees. The custodial wallet onboarding is a trade-off: it hides the blockchain (less education/awareness) but enables mass adoption (more users). Net positive for network metrics, neutral for sovereignty.

**Section G: Risk & Sustainability**
- ✓ Co-investment: Sellout put $130K+ of their own money into Phase 1
- ✓ Sustainability: Post-Phase 2, infrastructure maintained by Sellout as normal operating cost, funded by service fees
- ⚠ Market risk: Ticketing is competitive (Ticketmaster, Eventbrite, Dice). Sellout's niche is independent venues — defensible but limited
- ⚠ Anchor risk: Yellowstone Club is contracted but one client. Diversification depends on post-launch sales
- ⚠ Regulatory risk: NFT ticketing faces evolving regulations; anti-scalping controls may run afoul of resale-rights laws in some jurisdictions
- ✓ Technical risk mitigated: Anvil has deep Cardano experience; Phase 1 already works

**Section H: Subsidy-Loop & Dependency-Graph Check** — **Acceptable**. This funds a specific integration, not a permanent dependency. If Sellout fails, the CIP-68 ticketing contracts and marketplace code (if open-sourced) remain reusable. The revenue-share creates a sustainable ongoing relationship without requiring perpetual subsidy.

## Why DReps Are Leaning No (76.28%)

Despite strong fundamentals, this proposal faces headwinds:

1. **Amount skepticism** — Nearly 5M ADA feels large for a single-company integration, even with revenue-share
2. **"Fund a business" discomfort** — DReps struggle with treasury money going to a for-profit platform, even with repayment
3. **Custodial wallets** — The "hide blockchain from users" approach contradicts the self-sovereignty ethos some DReps value
4. **No open-source guarantee** — For 5M ADA, DReps want assurance the infrastructure is publicly reusable
5. **Competitive market** — Ticketing incumbents are entrenched; Cardano advantage is not yet proven at scale

The Committee's 3-0 Yes (with 4 abstentions) suggests no constitutional concerns, similar to the Builder DAO pattern.

---

## Strengths

1. **Working product already on mainnet** — Phase 1 is not a promise; it exists. This dramatically de-risks execution.
2. **Real user base** — 200K registered users, 350+ events/year. Not a "build it and they will come" proposal.
3. **Revenue-share repayment** — Treasury gets paid back (25% → 5%). This is rare and creates genuine alignment.
4. **Anchor deployment** — Yellowstone Club 2026 is contracted, not speculative. $6M+ projected on-chain volume.
5. **Detailed, honest budget** — Every FTE, every conference, every line item is specified. No hand-waving.
6. **Strong oversight** — Sundae Labs TRSC/PSSC + 6-member Oversight Committee + independent audit.
7. **Self-investment** — Sellout put $130K+ of their own money in. Skin in the game.

## Concerns

1. **No open-source commitment** — For ~5M ADA, the protocol code should be explicitly open-sourced under a permissive license
2. **Commercial nature** — Treasury is funding a for-profit platform's product development. Revenue-share helps but doesn't eliminate this tension.
3. **Custodial wallet trade-off** — 20K "new wallets" are custodial and invisible to users. Good for adoption metrics, questionable for Cardano education
4. **Scale risk** — 5M ADA for one integration. If this succeeds, will every enterprise integration ask for similar amounts?
5. **Market competition** — Ticketmaster, Eventbrite, Dice are entrenched. Sellout's independent-venue niche is defensible but not dominant.

## Vote Recommendation

**Vote: YES (with conditions)**

This proposal clears my public-value, execution, and accountability checks. It is the strongest commercial adoption proposal currently active — a working product, real users, contracted anchor deployment, detailed budget, milestone gating, independent oversight, and genuine revenue-share repayment. The $1.09M effective budget (at the $0.22 reference rate) is justified by the $6M+ projected on-chain volume and the 150K+ on-chain interactions.

However, I would support this more strongly if the applicants would commit to:
1. **Open-sourcing the CIP-68 ticketing contracts and marketplace smart contracts** under Apache 2.0 or MIT
2. **Publishing the security audit report publicly** (they commit to this in M3, which is good)
3. **A public commitment that the revenue-share dashboard remains accessible** even if Sellout is acquired or pivots

The DRep No vote (76.28%) reflects legitimate discomfort with funding a for-profit business. I share that discomfort — but the revenue-share structure, the working product, the self-investment, and the detailed accountability mechanisms make this a justifiable exception. Not every commercial proposal should pass, but this one has earned its ask.

**Rationale:** "Vote: Yes. This proposal delivers measurable on-chain activity from a working product with real users, contracted revenue, and a genuine repayment mechanism. The execution risk is low (Phase 1 is live), the oversight is strong, and the alignment with Vision 2030 adoption targets is direct. The amount is justified by the projected on-chain volume and the rarity of enterprise integrations at this maturity level."
