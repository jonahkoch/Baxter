**Acquisition Study Coinbase**

**Case Study 02 — Coinbase**

  

**Primary period:** 2018–2021 for the formative growth mechanisms, with current mechanisms noted where they illuminate how the model evolved.

  

The reason Coinbase is such a useful second case is that it combines **three different growth philosophies**:

  

1. **Robinhood-style friction reduction**
2. **Education as acquisition and activation**
3. **Crypto-native rewards and incentives**

  

And Coinbase gives us unusually good evidence because it disclosed its growth strategy in SEC filings.

  

⸻

  

**1. The central Coinbase growth proposition**

  

Coinbase’s early proposition was essentially:

  

**Make buying and owning Bitcoin understandable and easy for ordinary people.**

  

That sounds simple, but consider the alternative experience at the time:

  

Find an exchange → figure out how to fund it → understand wallets → understand Bitcoin → execute a trade.

  

Coinbase tried to turn that into:

  

**Create account → connect funding source → buy Bitcoin.**

  

The company’s 2021 S-1 explicitly described its retail platform as a “safe, trusted, and easy-to-use” way to discover and access the cryptoeconomy. At that time Coinbase reported approximately **43 million retail users**. 

  

So, like Robinhood:

  

**The first growth mechanism was simplification.**

  

⸻

  

**2. Coinbase’s most important insight: the user isn’t necessarily ready to buy**

  

This is where Coinbase diverges from Robinhood.

  

Robinhood could start with:

  

**“You want to invest.”**

  

Coinbase had a more difficult problem:

  

**“You might be curious about crypto, but you don’t understand it yet.”**

  

Coinbase explicitly identified a growing audience it called the **“crypto-curious.”**

  

In 2018, Coinbase described its research into people who were interested in crypto but wanted to understand **why crypto existed and how it worked** before participating. 

  

That’s a major growth insight.

  

The acquisition funnel wasn’t simply:

  

**Interested → Buy**

  

It became:

  

**Curious → Learn → Trust → Try → Buy**

  

⸻

  

**3. Education became part of the acquisition funnel**

  

Coinbase built **Coinbase Learn** around this problem.

  

The company provided:

  

- beginner guides
- practical explanations
- market information
- tutorials
- crypto education
- newsletters

  

The important thing is that education wasn’t treated as a completely separate corporate responsibility.

  

Coinbase explicitly said that education drives **activity and monetization**, and planned to use email and in-app notifications to educate users and drive engagement with products. 

  

This gives us a new mechanism:

  

**Education → trust → activation**

  

That’s different from Robinhood’s media strategy.

  

Robinhood’s content could bring someone into the investing ecosystem.

  

Coinbase’s education also had to overcome a **knowledge barrier inherent in the product itself**.

  

⸻

  

**4. Coinbase then turned education into an incentive**

  

This is the really important innovation.

  

**Coinbase Earn**

  

The basic mechanism:

  

**Learn about an asset → watch educational content → complete a quiz → receive crypto.**

  

Coinbase’s filings describe Learning Rewards as video-based educational content followed by a short quiz, with users receiving small crypto rewards for successful completion. 

  

So we get:

  

         CURIOSITY

             ↓

          EDUCATION

             ↓

           QUIZ

             ↓

           REWARD

             ↓

        OWN THE ASSET

             ↓

      POTENTIAL FIRST USE

  

This is fundamentally different from Robinhood’s free-stock referral.

  

Robinhood:

  

**Here’s a financial asset.**

  

Coinbase:

  

**Learn about this asset, then we’ll give you some.**

  

That’s a powerful distinction.

  

⸻

  

**5. The reward was also a product demonstration**

  

This is one of the most important lessons from Coinbase.

  

Suppose a user learns about an unfamiliar token.

  

They complete the lesson.

  

They receive $3 worth of that token.

  

Now something psychologically important has happened:

  

**They own something they just learned about.**

  

Instead of merely reading:

  

“Crypto assets can be transferred.”

  

the user can potentially experience:

  

**“I now own this asset.”**

  

The reward therefore serves three purposes:

  

**Education**

  

**activation**

  

**product sampling**

  

This is very similar to Robinhood’s free-stock mechanism, but much more tightly integrated with education.

  

⸻

  

**6. And the asset issuer helps pay for the acquisition**

  

This is where Coinbase Earn gets particularly interesting economically.

  

Coinbase disclosed that **Earn campaign revenue was $19.9 million in Q4 2021**, up 31% from Q3, and said growth came from both more retail users participating and larger amounts allocated by asset issuers for distribution. 

  

That creates an unusual model:

  

Asset issuer

      ↓

Funds distribution

      ↓

Coinbase

      ↓

Educational campaign

      ↓

User

      ↓

Learns + receives asset

  

So the **user acquisition cost isn’t necessarily borne entirely by Coinbase**.

  

The ecosystem participant that wants distribution can subsidize it.

  

That’s extremely relevant to DeFi.

  

⸻

  

**7. This is the first big difference from Robinhood**

  

Let’s put them side by side.

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
||**Robinhood**|**Coinbase**|
|Core barrier|Cost/complexity|Cost/complexity + knowledge|
|Acquisition proposition|Commission-free investing|Easy crypto access|
|Education|Important|**Central**|
|Free asset|Stock|Crypto|
|Quiz|No|**Yes**|
|Learn-to-earn|No|**Yes**|
|Asset issuer funding|No|**Yes**|
|Referral|Yes|Yes|
|Content|Snacks|Coinbase Learn/Bytes|
|Product sampling|Free stock|Earned crypto|

  

This is the beginning of our transition from:

  

**Fintech growth**

  

to

  

**Crypto ecosystem growth.**

  

⸻

  

**8. Referral: Coinbase has used several generations**

  

Coinbase launched a $25 referral program in 2015.

  

The structure was:

  

Existing user refers someone → new user buys at least $100 of Bitcoin → referrer gets $25 in Bitcoin.

  

Coinbase explicitly positioned this as a way for customers to help grow the Bitcoin ecosystem while earning money. 

  

Notice the important behavioral gate:

  

**The reward required a purchase.**

  

Not merely:

  

“Create an account.”

  

That gives us:

  

**Referral → Activation**

  

rather than:

  

**Referral → Registration**

  

⸻

  

**9. This principle survives today**

  

Coinbase’s current referral programs still use **qualifying activity** rather than simply rewarding account creation.

  

For example, its current referral terms require the referred user to sign up through the referral link and make a qualifying crypto purchase; certain Advanced referral programs instead use qualifying trading volume. 

  

That is a significant growth principle:

  

**Tie acquisition rewards to activation.**

  

And it gives us a very useful distinction for our future DeFi playbook:

  

**Bad growth metric**

  

**Cost per wallet created**

  

**Better**

  

**Cost per activated user**

  

**Better still**

  

**Cost per retained user**

  

**And ultimately**

  

**Cost per economically productive user**

  

⸻

  

**10. Coinbase developed a multi-product expansion loop**

  

Another fascinating element is what happens **after the first transaction**.

  

Coinbase doesn’t want:

  

Buy Bitcoin → done.

  

It wants:

  

Buy → stake → earn → spend → borrow → use → discover another asset → repeat.

  

Coinbase explicitly described the model as:

  

**Customers that start with us grow with us.**

  

Its 2021 filing described customers progressing through products including investing, staking, spending, earning, borrowing and transacting. 

  

This is a **product expansion loop**.

  

             First Product

                  ↓

               User

                  ↓

          Discover another

               product

                  ↓

             Use it

                  ↓

          Discover another

                  ↓

                ...

  

This is different from referral growth.

  

The user isn’t necessarily bringing another person.

  

**The user is growing deeper into the ecosystem.**

  

⸻

  

**11. Coinbase measured this as a funnel**

  

This is extremely useful for our research.

  

Coinbase separated:

  

**Verified Users**

  

People who have registered/verified.

  

from:

  

**Monthly Transacting Users — MTUs**

  

Retail users who actually transacted within the relevant 28-day period.

  

Coinbase explicitly described Verified Users as the **top of its customer acquisition funnel** and said its opportunity was to convert those users into MTUs through marketing and additional products. 

  

That’s essentially the framework we established in Chapter 1.

  

        VERIFIED USER

              ↓

          ACTIVATION

              ↓

            MTU

              ↓

       MORE PRODUCTS

              ↓

       MORE ENGAGEMENT

  

This is why I think Coinbase is such a good validation of our framework.

  

⸻

  

**12. Coinbase had an enormous organic component**

  

There’s another surprising piece.

  

In its 2021 S-1, Coinbase said that **more than 90% of its retail users since inception had found Coinbase organically or through word-of-mouth**, while it had spent less than 5% of net revenue on sales and marketing during 2020. 

  

That’s remarkable.

  

But again, we should be precise:

  

**This is Coinbase’s own reported attribution.**

  

We shouldn’t interpret “organic” as necessarily meaning “viral.”

  

Organic could include:

  

- direct traffic
- search
- word-of-mouth
- brand recognition
- unpaid content
- existing crypto community awareness

  

Nevertheless, the result is important:

  

**Coinbase’s acquisition wasn’t primarily dependent on paid advertising.**

  

⸻

  

**13. Education → product expansion**

  

Coinbase also reported that in Q2 2021:

  

- 27% of retail MTUs engaged with at least one non-investing product
- this represented approximately 2.4 million people
- the figure increased from 25% in Q1
- much of the incremental activity involved **Staking or Earn**. 

  

This gives us a powerful growth mechanism:

  

**Cross-sell as education.**

  

The user doesn’t necessarily need to leave the platform to discover a new use case.

  

Coinbase can say:

  

“You already own crypto. Here’s another thing you can do with it.”

  

That is extremely relevant to DeFi.

  

⸻

**16. Robinhood → Coinbase: the evolution**

  

This is the most important comparison so far.

  

**Robinhood**

  

**Make investing easy.**

  

↓

  

**Give people a reason to try.**

  

↓

  

**Give them a free piece of the product.**

  

↓

  

**Let users refer friends.**

  

**Coinbase**

  

**Make crypto understandable.**

  

↓

  

**Teach people.**

  

↓

  

**Give them a small piece of what they just learned about.**

  

↓

  

**Let them use it.**

  

↓

  

**Introduce additional crypto products.**

  

↓

  

**Let them refer others.**

  

The evolution is:

  

**Robinhood uses an asset to introduce the product.**

  

while:

  

**Coinbase uses education + an asset to introduce the ecosystem.**

  

⸻

  

**17. And now we reach the DeFi threshold**

  

This is where the next case studies become particularly interesting.

  

Coinbase still controls much of the onboarding experience.

  

The user largely thinks:

  

**“I’m using Coinbase.”**

  

But in DeFi, we eventually want:

  

**“I’m using an open financial network.”**

  

That’s a very different growth problem.

  

The protocol can’t necessarily rely on:

  

- centralized account creation
- centralized custody
- centralized customer relationship
- centralized notifications
- centralized product discovery

  

Instead, we start seeing mechanisms like:

  

**Airdrops**

  

**Liquidity mining**

  

**Quests**

  

**NFT achievements**

  

**On-chain reputation**

  

**Wallet-based identity**

  

**Governance**

  

**Referral contracts**

  

**Community distribution**

  

And that’s where I think **Uniswap** and **Optimism** become much more instructive.

  

⸻

  

**18. Coinbase’s most transferable lessons for Cardano**

  

If we’re thinking specifically about your Ethereum → Cardano DeFi onboarding concept, I would extract **six** mechanisms.

  

**1. Don’t assume users need to understand the technology first.**

  

Coinbase explicitly built around the “crypto-curious.”

  

**2. Education can be part of acquisition.**

  

Content doesn’t have to be an afterthought.

  

**3. Reward learning with the thing being learned.**

  

That’s the genius of Learn-to-Earn.

  

**4. Require meaningful activation.**

  

Referral rewards can require an actual purchase.

  

**5. Turn the first product into a gateway to the next.**

  

Buy → Earn → Stake → Spend → etc.

  

**6. Let ecosystem participants help finance acquisition.**

  

This one could be particularly interesting for Cardano.

  

Imagine a Cardano DeFi ecosystem where:

  

**DEX**

  

**lending protocol**

  

**stablecoin**

  

**wallet**

  

**NFT project**

  

**payment application**

  

each contributes to a shared onboarding/quest system.

  

Instead of every project independently paying for user acquisition:

  

**The ecosystem collectively funds the user’s journey.**

  

That’s starting to look less like conventional marketing and more like an **on-chain ecosystem growth infrastructure**.

**The key transition**

  

We now have:

  

**Case 01 — Robinhood**

  

**Friction removal + free product + referral**

  

↓

  

**Case 02 — Coinbase**

  

**Friction removal + education + learn-to-earn + referral + product progression**

  

↓

  

**Case 03 — Uniswap**

  

We’ll encounter something fundamentally different:

  

**Use the protocol → contribute economic activity → receive ownership/governance incentives.**

  

And then:

  

**Case 04 — Optimism**

  

**Learn → complete an on-chain action → prove it → receive achievement/reward → discover another application.**

  

That gives us a progression from:

  

**Fintech growth → crypto growth → DeFi growth → ecosystem growth.**

  

And I think **Uniswap is the right next case**, because it lets us examine the question that is central to your larger research:

  

**What happens when the growth incentive itself is a token—and the protocol has to decide whether the activity it is purchasing is actually worth the cost?**
