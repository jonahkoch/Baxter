## Summary

I vote **YES** to ratify the DRep Council Interim Charter v0.2. This document is an excellent foundational framework that strikes the right balance between structure and flexibility for a launch-phase organization. It correctly identifies the Council's role as a coordination and standards-setting body rather than a funding authority or voting bloc, and it embeds strong anti-capture and transparency principles from day one.

However, I submit the following **dissenting views** for the record. These do not change my vote, but I believe they represent material gaps that should be addressed in Charter v1 or through Council procedure.

---

## Dissenting View 1: Membership Criteria and Voting Timeline

### Historical Activity Requirement for Active Status

Section 7.3 defines Active Member status through quarterly participation metrics (100% voting, rationales, voluntary task contribution). I believe this should be supplemented with a **historical minimum activity requirement measured in epochs** — e.g., evidence of sustained on-chain DRep activity over a defined lookback period prior to admission or renewal.

**Rationale:**
- **Sybil resistance:** A minimum epoch-based activity threshold raises the cost of creating sock-puppet DReps for Council infiltration.
- **Longevity bias:** Sunk cost and loss aversion are well-documented behavioral mechanisms that encourage continued participation. Members who have invested meaningful time into their DRep identity are more likely to remain engaged and act in the ecosystem's long-term interest.
- **Signal quality:** Epoch-based history is harder to fake than snapshot participation metrics and provides a more reliable indicator of genuine governance engagement.

### Voting Window: Extend from 7 Days to 15–30 Days

Section 8.6 and Section 11 establish a 7-day voting period for operational decisions, official recommendations, and charter amendments. I believe this is insufficient for meaningful deliberation on substantive matters and should be **extended to a minimum of 15 days, with up to 30 days for complex or high-stakes decisions**.

**Rationale:**
- **Due diligence gap:** The merit of pre-submission consultation (Section 11) is acknowledged, but the final draft is the only document that members can truly judge. Relying on pre-vetted assumptions about what will appear in the final submission introduces verification risk.
- **Real-world constraints:** Council members hold responsibilities to employment, family, and their own DRep constituencies. A 7-day window forces rushed judgment on matters that affect the entire Cardano ecosystem.
- **Decision quality:** Good governance requires time for ingestion, analysis, discussion, and sleep. Compressed timelines favor reactive voting over reflective deliberation.

**Alternative for urgent matters:** Where faster decision-making is genuinely required, the Council could adopt a lower-stakes format (e.g., a signaling pool or straw poll) that does not carry the weight of an official recommendation but allows rapid coordination.

## Dissenting View 2: Scope Should Explicitly Include DRep Onboarding and Member Acquisition

Section 6.1 lists the Council's primary scope for 2026. I believe **DRep onboarding, ecosystem outreach, and member acquisition should be explicitly added** to this list, not treated as an implicit byproduct of other activities.

**Rationale:**
- **Survivorship bias:** Those of us who found the DRep Council organically cannot assume others will be equally motivated or well-networked. Passive discovery is not a growth strategy.
- **Network effects:** The Council's legitimacy and output quality scale with participation. A small, insular group risks becoming an echo chamber rather than a representative coordination layer.
- **Value proposition:** The Council should become a **Key Opinion Leader (KOL) for the Cardano ecosystem and DReps** — a pragmatic, nimble, and effective macro-oracle that synthesizes information and connects dots across the governance landscape.
- **AG/OG as catalyst:** Alpha Growth (AG) and Original Growth (OG) represent the kind of high-visibility, high-impact coordination opportunities that can demonstrate the Council's value to prospective members. Without an explicit mandate to grow membership, this critical work will be deprioritized in favor of immediate procedural tasks.

The charter already states that the Council is "a body that can produce public recommendations, rationales, feedback, standards, and reports." I would add: **"and actively recruit, onboard, and support new DRep members to expand the Council's representativeness and impact."**

## Conclusion

The Interim Charter v0.2 is a strong enough foundation to begin operations. My YES vote reflects confidence in the document's core architecture: the anti-capture provisions, the log₁₀ voting model, the clear delineation of what the Council is and is not, and the commitment to review and replace this charter by year-end.

My dissenting views are offered constructively. I hope they will inform the Charter v1 drafting process and interim Council procedures as we learn by doing.
